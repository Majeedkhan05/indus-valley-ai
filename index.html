01 — About this system

A focused AI for a single,
extraordinary civilization.

Most AI is a generalist. Indus Valley AI isn't — it answers only on the Indus / Harappan world. That constraint is the point: scoped knowledge, cited sources, no hallucinated empires.

  • 01
    Domain-locked.

    Out-of-scope queries are politely declined and redirected back to the civilization. No drift, no invented history.

  • 02
    Source-aware.

    Answers reference real publications — Yajnadevam's Indus Inscriptions, the Authority Structure paper, indusscript.net corpus, ASI reports.

  • 03
    Interpretive, not declarative.

    The Indus script is undeciphered. The system flags consensus, debate, and open questions — never one confidently false answer.

  • 04
    Free, in service of research.

    Built by AI Hub for students, scholars, and the curious — anywhere in the world.

06 — Chronology

From Mehrgarh to collapse.

  1. 7000–3300 BCE

    Mehrgarh — the seedbed

    Neolithic farming, dentistry, cotton, terracotta. Earliest evidence of South Asian sedentism in Balochistan.

  2. 3300–2600 BCE

    Early Harappan

    Regional cultures (Ravi, Hakra, Sothi-Siswal) crystallize. Proto-script. First seals. Pre-urban.

  3. 2600–1900 BCE

    Mature Harappan — the great cities

    Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Dholavira, Lothal, Rakhigarhi peak. Standardized weights, drainage, seal-script, long-distance trade with Mesopotamia.

  4. 1900–1300 BCE

    Late Harappan — transformation

    Cities depopulate. Drying of the Ghaggar-Hakra (Saraswati) system. Material culture continues in eastern transformations into Cemetery H, Painted Grey Ware.

08 — The undeciphered script

~4,000 inscriptions. Still unread.

The Indus script consists of roughly 400 distinct signs across an estimated ~4,000 short inscriptions — most under five signs. It remains one of the great open puzzles of human prehistory.

Sign count

~400

Distinct signs catalogued. Too many for a pure alphabet, too few for a pure logography.

Average inscription

~5 signs

Most are short — names, titles, or trade tags. The longest known is 26 signs.

Reading direction

RTL

Right-to-left, evidenced by sign-cramping at line ends and seal/sealing pairs.

Leading hypothesis

Logo-syllabic

A mixed system combining word-signs and syllable-signs — though the underlying language remains contested.

        09 — World trade · 2500 BCE

        The first global civilization.

        Indus carnelian beads have been excavated in the Royal Tombs of Ur. Indus weights regulated commerce in the Persian Gulf. Mesopotamians knew them as Meluhha.

        Indus →

        Mesopotamia

        Carnelian beads, ivory, hardwoods, cotton. Identified in cuneiform texts as Meluhha. Seals found at Ur, Kish, Susa.

        Indus →

        Oman / Magan

        Copper-tin trade entrepôt. Indus-style pottery and weights at Ras al-Jinz, Salut, Tell Abraq.

        Indus →

        Bahrain / Dilmun

        The maritime hub of Persian Gulf trade. Indus weight standard used; round Persian-Gulf seals fuse Indus and local iconography.

        Indus →

        Central Asia / BMAC

        Lapis lazuli sourced via Shortugai, an Indus outpost on the Oxus. Steatite and ivory north into the Bactria-Margiana complex.

        10 — Research foundation

        Sources the AI actually reads.

        A focused, citation-grade reading list. The system is grounded in these works and openly admits when a question lies outside them.

        1. 01

          Yajnadevam — Indus Inscriptions

          A computational and statistical re-reading of the Indus corpus, arguing for a logo-syllabic system phonetically tied to early Indo-Aryan.

          Statistical decipherment · 2023

        2. 02

          Authority Structure & the Evolution of Early Writing Systems

          A comparative paper on how administrative complexity shaped writing in Sumer, Egypt, and the Indus — and what the Indus script's sign-economy implies about its political structure.

          Comparative epigraphy · 2024

        3. 03

          indusscript.net corpus

          The open online catalogue of Indus inscriptions — sign-by-sign, seal-by-seal. The reference dataset for any quantitative work.

          Open dataset

        4. 04

          Indus-Seal-Dataset (4,000+ seals)

          The visual dataset powering the seal-gallery on this site — symbolic and textual annotation pairs for each seal.

          Open dataset

        5. 05

          ASI excavation reports — Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, Lothal

          Archaeological Survey of India primary reports — particularly R. S. Bisht (Dholavira) and Vasant Shinde (Rakhigarhi 2018 aDNA study).

          Primary archaeology

          Kenoyer · Possehl · Parpola

          The standard secondary literature: J. M. Kenoyer's Ancient Cities of the Indus, G. L. Possehl's The Indus Civilization, A. Parpola's Deciphering the Indus Script.

        11 — Indian heritage spotlight

        Dholavira, Lothal & the Indian Indus.

        More than 1,000 mature-Harappan sites lie in modern India — most still under-excavated. The AI surfaces what we already know, and what we still don't.

        Dholavira · Gujarat · UNESCO 2021

        The only Harappan city built almost entirely of stone. Three-tier division (citadel · middle town · lower town), 16 reservoirs, the world's earliest known signboard — ten large signs over the citadel gate.

        Lothal · Gujarat

        The oldest known dock in the world. A planned port-town with bead-making workshops, a fire-altar enigma, and direct evidence of Persian Gulf trade.

        Rakhigarhi · Haryana

        Possibly the largest Harappan site by area. The 2018 Shinde et al. aDNA study from a Rakhigarhi skeleton found no Steppe ancestry in mature Harappan individuals — reframing the Indo-Aryan migration debate.

        Kalibangan · Rajasthan

        "Black bangles" — earliest ploughed field, fire-altars, a distinct citadel-and-lower-town plan on the dry Ghaggar-Hakra channel.

        Banawali · Surkotada · Chanhudaro

        Smaller but rich: Banawali's ploughed field and toy plough, Surkotada's horse-bone debate, Chanhudaro's beadmaking precision.

        Why it matters

        The Indus is foundational Indian history. Recovering it — accurately, free of nationalism on either side — is a public good. That's the goal here.
